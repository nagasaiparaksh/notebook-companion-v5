# Review: intro-v2 (stone → leaves → paper → printing → typing → touch → voice → AI)

## Creative calls
- One object carries through every era: carvings → palm-leaf script → parchment → printed page → typewriter paper → screen →
  laptop → tablet. Each era is a morph or a caused cut (blur swap, whip pan, page flying into the lens), never a crossfade of worlds.
- The palm-leaf manuscript (an Indian writing medium) was chosen for "leaves".
- The cartoon student is the same rig as v1; the line he speaks ("Explain my notes in simple words.") is the demo's user voice
  (Gnani Devansh), so the cartoon and the real demo are one person. Narrator: Gnani Prakash (male), as asked.
- Timeline scene uses real stills from this film for its seven panels, and starts inside the frozen VOICE frame (pull-back).
- Scene 9 opens the *real* app page out of the waveform (no mock UI); scene 10 widens it to the full app, which steps aside for the
  title and returns as the exact first frame of the voice demo.

## Measured vs looked at
- `check`: passes; kept warning `composition_file_too_large` on modern.html (one continuous shot on purpose).
- Looked at: every scene's key frames, all seams, the phone-width labels. Narration placement measured with silencedetect.
- Final film loudness measured −16.9 LUFS, peak −1.5 dBFS. Sound was measured, not heard.
- No music: HeyGen sign-in needed for the catalog.

## Weakest shots / with more time
- The cave carver is a silhouette with a plain round head; a profile face and a second hand pose would sell it more.
- The scribe in "paper" is only a hand; a shoulder/face over-the-shoulder shot would add a person to that era.
- Timeline panels are small (224 px) at 1080p; fine on desktop, small on phones.
